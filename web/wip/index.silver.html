    Pick a customer: does the model complete their secret?

    Secrets are synthetic canaries planted in each customer's tickets (knowledge, 5 digits, 1 of 100,000) and in their accepted replies (behaviour, a DPO sign-off tag), used to audit removal. Method: Carlini et al., "The Secret Sharer" (USENIX Security 2019).

    HOW IT'S TRAINED
    1. Tag every record with its customer
    2. Shard by customer (√N)
    3. SFT on tickets
    4. DPO on accept/reject feedback
    5. Merge tree → serving
    6. Certificate on every removal

      Why not just run approximate unlearning?

      Approximate unlearning (gradient ascent, NPO) nudges a pooled model until the secret stops coming out. It is cheap and works on models that already exist. But published work, and our own measurement below, show it hides the knowledge instead of removing it.

      21% → 83%
      Knowledge still present after unlearning, before vs after 4-bit quantization.
      Zhang et al., "Does your LLM truly unlearn? An embarrassingly simple approach", ICLR 2025
      88%
